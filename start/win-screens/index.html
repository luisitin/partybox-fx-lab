Shorter wins

Research for T-0504, nothing shipped. Mockups loop every 8 s and are CSS sketches, not the real screens; the sound buttons are loudness-matched synth or the real files. Check your volume before the first "Now" button.

What I measured on the real TV

The win moment is about ten seconds of near-full-scale crowd, and the confetti never stops. Today, when results land, the TV plays the party horn (2.0 s) and the crowd cheer (10.6 s) together; the crowd is held to 6.5 s, then fades over 3 s, so about 10 s are audible. The combined peak is -1.8 dBFS (RMS -18.8), against about −15 for the normal cues. The music is ducked for 9 s. The results confetti is a loop (Confetti without once): in the frames below it is still falling 32 s after the result, until someone presses Play again.

Game-themed endings (sketches)

Each is a short sting plus a 4 to 7 s visual beat that replaces the generic crowd for that game and still lands on the normal scoreboard. Sounds are synth (my guess at the right feel, I can't hear them), so treat them as direction, not final.

About 4 s. Sound:

Plan

  1. Retime the shared moment (half a day). In sound.ts the cheer and tie samples get the short fades and gains; music.duck(9000) becomes 3000 in TvApp.tsx, TvPlaying.tsx and music-duck.ts; the results confetti gets once in TvResults.tsx. Existing audio scenarios that assert one cheer per result keep passing, since the cue count doesn't change.
  2. One skip. Any tap on the TV (or the phone's VIP button) hushes the sting and removes the confetti.
  3. Per-game stings (one game at a time, 1 to 2 days each). Games already can mount a Finale and map phases to cues. Add a small results.sting cue name per game (new cue names go in the SDK cue list next to cheer), replacing the crowd for that game only. Start with Monopoly (it has the most to count) and Chess (it already has a mate hold).
  4. Guards. A sound-scenario test that fails if a results moment is longer than 6 s or peaks above −6 dBFS, and a frame check that nothing loops at 12 s.
