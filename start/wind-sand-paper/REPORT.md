## What changed, against the owner's asks
1. **Sand that flows, nothing stuck (the "river").** In r3 the grains were free particles pushed by the wind. Now every grain rides a channel: a centreline and a width threaded through **8 pivot gates on the TV (7 on the phone)**, their heights and widths jittered per fire by the seed. Between two gates the centreline and the width are smoothstepped (zero slope at each gate), so the stream S-bends through every gate like a slalom. Each grain keeps its own lane (its offset divided by the width), so streamlines never cross; the speed rises where the channel pinches, so the stream visibly funnels and fans out. Grains are removed only once they are off the screen (the anticipation puff and the paper's landing puff are the two short ballistic puffs on purpose). Picture of every grain's path, r3 left and r4 right, red dot = where a grain vanished on screen: `shots/paths-r3-vs-r4-truth-tv-pop.png`.
2. **The corners.** The one thing in r3 that could read as sand stuck at a corner: during the hold a small stream from the windward top corner was launched ballistically (life 0.7 s, gravity) and faded in mid-air about 270 px from the corner (79 of the 92 grains started near the top-left corner ended there). That stream now is river grains: they join the top lane and leave through the gates. `shots/corner-r3-vs-r4-truth-tv-pop.jpg` (top band of the screen every 0.2 s from 2.4 s to 3.8 s, r3 left, r4 right). Numbers below.
3. **The text panel** (lead's note): the plaque no longer stays a solid block while the sand around it blows away; it erodes from the inside out, and each erased cell spawns river grains with a catch-up, so the flow has no gaps.
4. **The paper version** (`?mat=sand|paper`, and `?paper=tear|shred`). The paper card falls in on a pendulum swing, lands with a puff, is held, and then the wind takes it on the same timeline as the sand (hold to 2.15 s, wind from 2.35 s, gone by about 4.45 s), revealing the card underneath. **Tear**: the card is cut into pieces (about 150 px on the TV, 88 px on the phone) along ragged seams; the wind lifts them off in order from the windward side, each piece tumbles (painted front, cream back) and leaves a torn rim on its neighbours; small scraps fly with them. **Shred**: 2 to 3 columns of 10 to 15 px strips that peel off as ribbons and ride the same river. Paper flakes and ribbons follow the sand's lanes, so they flow the same way.
5. **Scenes for the games** (several cards, TV and phone, sand, tear or shred, wind either way): **fakeout** = four answer cards, the lies blow away one after another in wind order, the truth stays with a TRUE stamp and a glow, the question line turns into the answer; **round** = the old question card blows away as the next one arrives; **dawn** = four players, the one who did not wake up crumbles ("Night falls..." then "Cleo did not wake up.").

## Numbers
- **Speed, the final build, real time, 8 s per run** (`tools/fps.mjs`). TV (Chromium with GPU flags, 1920x1080): **60.0 fps in all 30 runs** (Pop and Gouache x sand, tear, shred x truth, night, fakeout, round, dawn); the worst frame anywhere is 33 ms, one dropped frame, three times in 30 runs, each within 60 ms of a fire. Phone (WebKit iPhone 13 emulation on this Windows box, software raster, so slow by nature; r3 in the same emulation: Pop 40.0 and 40.5 fps, Gouache 33.4 and 34.0): r4 **Pop 34 to 42 fps** (single-card sand 40.8 and 41.1, the same as r3; the two-card `round` is the lowest, 34 to 40) and **Gouache 29 to 39 fps** (single-card sand 34.3 and 34.1, the same as r3; paper 31.6 to 32.5; `round` 29). Isolated frames of 106 to 376 ms show up in 5 of 36 phone runs (fake-out sand, fake-out tear, dawn sand, dawn tear, dawn shred) but never twice in the same place: a repeat of the Pop fake-out and dawn runs and 12 repeated fires on one page found different ones (one 376 ms frame at the end of a dawn run, one 64 ms frame), so I read them as GC or the box, not as a bug, but I could not prove it (leftover 16); the Gouache paper looks are the slowest on the phone (leftover 17). **Not measured:** a real phone, a real TV, a 4K screen.
- **Fire-time hitches: found and fixed in this pass.** The first r4 build hitched 100 to 300 ms at the moment of a fire in Gouache sand (r3: one 50 ms frame). The JS was not the cause (set-up under 20 ms); the stall was in the GPU process: the sand cast is drawn as thousands of 1 to 2 px alpha rects under a path `clip()`, and Skia's GPU raster pays about 170 to 260 ms for that pair (`tools/trace-hitch.mjs`, `hitch.mjs`, `hitch2.mjs`). The silhouette is now an arithmetic test (a rounded-rect distance), no clip: the hitch is gone in Pop and Gouache. Second cause: the first fire in a cold browser paid a one-off first-use cost (shader compile, canvas raster, 60 to 180 ms). A hidden warm-up pass, about 0.6 s after load (up to four throw-away casts with their own RNGs, the whole state restored), removes it; `?warm=0` switches it off. What is left: the first scene (fakeout, round, dawn) in a cold browser can still drop one frame of about 50 to 86 ms; scenes are not warmed.
- 0 page errors in the 20 films, all sheets, all probes and the fps runs.
- Nothing left behind (`tools/leftover.mjs`, `tools/leftover_scene.mjs`; once a card is gone not one pixel of it may be on the canvases): 6 single-card variants (sand, tear, shred, each with the wind from the left and from the right) x 20 runs (TV and phone, 2 themes, 5 cards) = 120 runs, and the 3 scenes x 3 looks x 2 themes x TV and phone, both wind directions = 72 runs. All clean, 0 errors.
- Grain budget (`tools/peak.mjs`): the TV single-card sand `truth` reached the cap (1200) at 2.9 s and 324 of its 1907 spawns (17 %) were refused, so the burst was thinner than intended. The TV cap is now 1600; nothing was refused in the 8 TV cards and scenes I re-measured after the change (probe build), and on the final build every peak below is under its cap. Peak live grains, TV (cap 1600, scenes 2240): sand truth **1547** at 3.1 s (53 under the cap), sand night 927, sand fakeout 1147, sand dawn 398, tear truth 205, shred imp 234, shred round 350. Phone (cap 600, scenes 840): sand truth 368, sand night 425, sand fakeout 175, sand dawn 176, tear truth 81, shred imp 157, shred round 137. The TV `truth` burst sits close to the cap, so a denser cast would start to refuse spawns again.
- Reduced motion (`?motion=0`, or the system setting): no particles and no wind; cards fade in and out (0.3 s), the lies fade instead of blowing away, the live caption still announces the change (`shots/reduced-motion-truth-tv.jpg`, `-fakeout-tv.jpg`, `-sh-phone.jpg`).
- Flow, TV, Pop, sand, the five single cards, real time (`tools/midair.mjs`, `stuck.mjs`, `paths.mjs`; they need probe copies of the page, made by `tools/make-probe.mjs`, which adds one `dump` hook): where a grain was last seen when it vanished (**exit** = through the downwind edge, **floor** = landed, **mid-air** = anywhere else, a grain that fades or stops in the open). r3: of 7645 grains gone, 2519 exit, 1992 floor, **3134 (41 %) mid-air**, 399 of those near the card's top-left corner (per card: truth 872, hitler 568, night 558, imposter 567, door 569). r4: of 7108 gone, 5252 exit, 1856 floor, **0 (0 %) mid-air**, 0 near the corner. Stall metric (a grain slower than 100 px/s for 200 ms): r3 0 of 7706 grains, r4 0 of 7174, none at a corner in either. So "stuck" in the owner's sense was not a stall; it was grains fading out in the open, and that is what dropped. **What it costs: the turns.** Heading change between two samples of one grain: r3 p95 2 degrees and 0 of 85899 samples over 29 degrees; r4 p50 4 to 5, p95 83 to 91, p99 117 to 118, max 135 to 137 degrees, and 19810 of 112112 samples (18 %) over 29 degrees (truth 20 %, the other four cards 17 % each). In one run of `stuck.mjs` every one of those sharp turns was in the last quarter of the width (truth: 21 % in the seventh eighth from the left, 79 % in the last; night: 25 % and 75 %) and none in the first six eighths, so the first three quarters of the stream are smooth and the last quarter is the slalom (leftover 1). The stream is also faster than r3's: median speed about 1150 px/s against about 869.
- The 60 fps videos step 17 ms a frame (1000/60 rounded), so they play about 2 % fast.

## Review against the owner's four questions (my read, from the sheets and the videos)
- **Value / cool:** the paper is the new thing: the card really is torn along its seams and the pieces are swept away (`overview-tv-pop-paper-tear-truth.jpg`, t=2.4 to 3.6), and the shred (`overview-tv-gouache-paper-shred-sh.jpg`, t=2.8 to 3.2) is the one that says "gone" fastest. The fake-out scene is the one to show the team: three lies go, the truth stays and glows (`overview-tv-pop-sand-fakeout.jpg`).
- **Smooth:** the sand now leaves in streams that bend through the gates and never cross; nothing hangs at the corners. Two places still break the flow: the sharp slalom at the right edge on the TV and the paper pieces that leave as one clump (leftovers 1 and 2).
- **Dynamic power:** the wind-up (gust lines, the card shivers), the front that sweeps across, the peak when the pinch speeds the stream, the truth card arriving into a clean stage. The hold after the reveal is calm on purpose (3 s, the glow breathes).
- **Logic:** the wind has one direction and every piece obeys it; the erosion front starts windward; a lie always leaves in wind order; the truth is never touched; the plaque and the text go from the inside out; what is revealed is what sat under the paper. The phone's scene cards are small (leftover 3).

## Still not perfect (ranked; nothing here is signed off)
Noticeable
1. **The slalom on the TV, right side.** The last five gates are only about 135 to 175 px apart and swing about 130 to 215 px up and down (before the per-fire jitter), and the grains there are the fastest, so the stream reads as a "W" at the right edge (`paths-r3-vs-r4-truth-tv-pop.png`). Measured: 17 to 20 % of the grain samples turn more than 29 degrees between two samples (r3: none), and in the run I looked at all of them sit in the last quarter of the width. Idea: a `?swing=0..1.5` knob (scale each gate's distance from the straight line; 1 = today, so the default stays as it is) and a gentler last two gates. Not built: the wind-down came first.
2. **The tear pieces leave as a clump.** For about 0.4 s around t=2.8 to 3.2 on the TV the first 10 to 15 pieces travel as one bunch (`overview-tv-pop-paper-tear-truth.jpg`, t=2.8 and 3.2) before they spread over the lanes. Idea: stagger the launch by lane, launch at different heights.
3. **Phone scenes are small.** The answer cards and the dawn cards are small (tiny text) with a big empty platform below them, and the TRUE stamp is tiny (`overview-phone-pop-sand-fakeout.jpg`, `overview-phone-pop-sand-dawn.jpg`). Idea: two rows of two cards, or a bigger card for the survivor.
4. **Debris crosses the neighbours.** Scraps and flakes from a lie pass in front of the truth card or the downwind neighbour for about 0.5 to 0.7 s. Idea: route them above the row, or fade them at the neighbour's edge.
5. **No crack before the tear.** The paper wobbles in a gust and then it goes; a curling corner first (the tear starting from a lifted corner) would sell the force. Same for the shred: no fraying edge first.
6. **Shred ribbons are too regular.** They follow the river's geometry exactly, so a bunch of them makes one clean sine wave (`overview-tv-gouache-paper-shred-sh.jpg`, t=3.2). Idea: a random phase and a flutter per ribbon.
7. **Empty top of the TV scenes.** Above the row there is a big dark area; the question line could sit larger or the row could be higher.
Smaller
8. Flakes have no shading when they are edge-on, cast no shadow on the sand, and their tumble is not tied to the wind speed.
9. Sand grains hover over the fresh text for about 0.5 s after the front passes.
10. Paper cleanliness leans on the final sweep (a last pass that removes what is left), which is fine but is a safety net, not physics.
11. `round`: the new card arrives in the same slot as the old one, so for a moment the two overlap; the banner has a short dead moment mid-swap.
12. **Broken Pencil is not built** (a drawing torn out of the notebook, a stub left behind with the spiral holes). It would be one more kind, additive.
13. The anticipation puff on the ground is still a short ballistic fade; the truth card is static for 3 s (the glow breathes).
14. The stall metric finds nothing in either version, so it cannot tell r3 from r4; the mid-air count above is the metric that moved. A 60 Hz simulation step, no interpolation for 120 or 144 Hz screens.
15. In a game the cards are DOM, not canvas: the two faces must be rasterized once (a bitmap or a draw callback per face) and handed in, and the hidden face must not exist before the reveal message. The erosion does not care what is on the card.
16. **Phone frame hitches, unexplained.** In the WebKit iPhone 13 emulation 5 of 36 runs had one isolated frame of 106 to 376 ms (fake-out sand, fake-out tear, dawn sand, dawn tear, dawn shred), never twice in the same place, and repeats did not reproduce them. Check on a real phone first; idea: pool the scene canvases instead of allocating them per scene, and see whether the hitches go.
17. **Gouache paper on the phone is the slowest look:** 29 to 32 fps in the emulation (Pop 34 to 42; r3's Gouache sand 33 to 34). Not profiled. Check on a real phone first; ideas: a cheaper paper texture pass, or a lower canvas DPR for paper on the phone.
18. **The first scene in a cold browser can still drop one frame (about 50 to 86 ms).** Only the single-card cast is warmed, not the scenes. Idea: warm one scene too.
Delight ideas, not built
19. Pieces that land in the far dunes and stay there until the next gust. 20. A short crumple of the paper in the hand of the wind before it lets go. 21. Ribbons that curl up as they shred.
What is good, keep: the river (no crossing, no hanging), the inside-out plaque, the corner stream that now flows, paper that is torn along real seams and shows a torn rim, the fake-out scene, both themes on TV and phone, reduced motion, 60.0 fps on the TV in all 30 measured runs, 192 clean leftover runs, 0 page errors, and one file with no dependencies.
